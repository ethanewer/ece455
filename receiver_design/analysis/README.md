Gain is from J1 (`receiver_in`) through the AD8421 module (G = 100) and the two-section RC anti-aliasing filter to ADS1256 AIN0, with AIN1 at GND. The 11 kΩ filter resistance and the ADS1256's unbuffered input impedance lower the in-band gain to about 85 V/V. `make verify` checks |Z_in|, noise, interference, and alias rejection at temperature, supply, battery, trim, and tolerance corners. The test source is 10 µV behind a 30 kΩ fixture that stands in for the externally tuned sensor. Coil tuning and damping are outside this receiver model.

## Noise

Input-referred to the source EMF, integrated from 1.6 to 2.2 kHz with ngspice `.noise`, plus the ADS1256's 10.3 µV RMS converter noise (PGA 1, 30 kSPS, buffer off) referred through the minimum in-band gain, 1.01 nV/√Hz. The noise figure compares the in-band total with the source resistance's thermal noise alone. The frequency CRB uses the untuned coil's EMF-referred density at 1876 Hz as a white floor, a 1.5 s record at 30 kSPS after a 200 ms blank, and T2* = 0.95 s. Compare it with the 1 nT target. An external tuned capacitor raises the FID and coil noise together, which lowers the receiver's share of the noise. The AD8421 model uses the datasheet's maximum 3.2 nV/√Hz input voltage noise and typical 60 nV/√Hz output and 200 fA/√Hz current noise. It omits the module's BIAS/REF path noise, so measure the floor on the bench.
